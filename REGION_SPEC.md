## 1. Geographic Scope & Thermodynamic Climate Profile

- **Target States (9 States):** New York, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, New Jersey, New Hampshire, Vermont, Maine, Rhode Island.
- **IECC / ASHRAE 90.1 Climate Zones:**
  - **Zone 5A (Cold - Humid):** Boston, Philadelphia, New York City, Hartford, Providence, lower Hudson Valley. Severe winter freezing (HDD65: 5,400 to 6,500), deep frost lines (36 to 48 inches), heavy coastal nor'easters with freezing rain and wind-driven snow.
  - **Zone 6A (Cold - Severe):** Upstate New York (Buffalo, Rochester, Albany, Adirondacks), Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine. Intense lake-effect snowstorms (60 to 100+ inches annually), ground snow loads exceeding 50 to 90 PSF, and prolonged sub-zero temperatures down to -20°F to -35°F.
  - **Zone 4A (Coastal Maritime):** Long Island, coastal New Jersey. High atmospheric humidity combined with winter freeze/thaw cycles and salt-air atmospheric corrosion.
- **Core Building Physics & Structural Challenges:**
  - **Aging Stone Rubble & Fieldstone Foundations:** 100 to 250-year-old unreinforced fieldstone and lime mortar foundation walls experiencing capillary bulk water infiltration, lime mortar efflorescence/degradation, and frost heave.
  - **Balloon-Frame Heritage & Uninsulated Cavity Thermal Chimneys:** Pre-1930s balloon framing with open rafter bays, horsehair plaster walls, and continuous convective loops leaking thousands of BTUs per hour into uninsulated attic knee walls.
  - **High Fuel Oil Costs & Cold-Climate Heat Pump Decarbonization:** Millions of homes reliant on expensive No. 2 heating oil and hydronic cast-iron baseboards; transition to high-efficiency cold-climate air-to-water or ductless inverter heat pumps facing electrical panel capacity limits (100-amp service upgrades).
  - **Ice Damming & Coastal Nor'easter Wind Loads:** Rapid snow accumulation on steep gables and saltbox roofs followed by heat loss from poorly insulated attic planes, creating massive eave ice dams that tear historic copper gutters and rot fascia boards.
  - **Historic Preservation Commission (HDC) Compliance:** Tight municipal and state historic district guidelines restricting exterior cladding replacement, window modernizations, and exterior continuous insulation additions, requiring advanced interior hygrothermal retrofit detailing.
  - **Attic Kneewall Thermal & Pressure Boundaries:** Traditional Cape Cod and New England Saltbox architecture with unconditioned knee wall storage spaces leaking cold exterior air behind second-floor bedroom knee walls.

## 2. Dominant Regional Architectural Vernacular & Housing Archetypes

1. **New England Colonial & Saltbox (1690–1800):** Massive central brick chimneys, heavy timber timber-frame construction with mortise-and-tenon joinery, steep roof pitches, uninsulated fieldstone basements.
2. **Victorian & Queen Anne (1880–1910):** Intricate balloon framing, multi-faceted rooflines, turret valleys, uninsulated decorative wood shake cladding, unsealed pocket doors functioning as air leakage channels.
3. **Brownstone & Rowhouse Masonry (1850–1920):** New York City, Brooklyn, Philadelphia, Boston. Multi-story load-bearing party walls of soft red clay brick, porous brown sandstone facades (brownstone), flat roofs with tin/asphalt membranes and parapet copings.
4. **Classic New England Cape Cod (1920–1960):** 1.5-story framing with uninsulated side kneewalls, low collar ties, dormer window valleys, drafty unconditioned crawlspaces under first-floor bump-outs.
5. **Mid-Century Tri-Level & Split-Entry (1955–1975):** Uninsulated concrete foundation walls half-embedded in earth, drafty cantilevers over garages, failing galvanized domestic plumbing.
