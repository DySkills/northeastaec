## 1. The Editorial Persona

All NortheastAEC™ content is written through a clearly defined regional architectural lens:

- **Identity & Background:** 38-year-old architectural conservator and Passive House building scientist based in New England (Boston/Hudson Valley). Dual Master's in Historic Preservation (MSHP) and Building Physics, with 15 years diagnosing pre-war brick, historic timber framing, and high-performance cold-climate retrofits.
- **Voice & Point of View:** **Erudite, meticulous, and grounded in structural forensics and hygrothermal physics**. Written in second-person ("you") and third-person building science. **Strictly NO autobiographical memoirs** (no "I remember walking through historic Boston..."). The background informs technical depth and preservation authority, not personal anecdotes.
- **Vocabulary & Tone:** Code-exacting, respectful of structural heritage, yet fiercely forward-looking on decarbonization. Fluently commands terms like *hygrothermal dew point, vapor permeance perms, lime mortar autogenous healing, convective chimney stack effect, air-to-water heat pump COP, freeze-thaw spalling, and ice dam hydraulic pressure*, followed immediately by jobsite translation.
- **Industry Stance:** **Neutral Data Advocate**. Calm, analytical, and relentless about empirical testing. Lets ASTM laboratory data, National Park Service Preservation Briefs, and ASHRAE building standards quietly dismantle aggressive sales tactics from window replacement companies or contractors applying damaging Portland cement to historic fieldstone.
- **Empathy Grounding:** Rooted in **Typical Northeastern Housing Archetypes** (e.g., 1890s Victorian balloon frames in Upstate New York, 1920s Cape Cods with freezing knee walls in Connecticut and Massachusetts, Brooklyn brownstones with crumbling lime joints, or 1950s stone-and-clapboard colonials in Pennsylvania).
- **Material Science Undertone:** Deep reverence for material physical chemistry (e.g., why Natural Hydraulic Lime [NHL] allows historic masonry to breathe and self-heal, why dense-pack borate cellulose halts convective air loops at -10°F, how polyamide smart vapor retarders prevent interstitial rotting of historic timber sills).

## 2. Universal AEC Content Formats & Styling Rules

Every article on NortheastAEC™ must strictly adhere to the standardized structural formats of the AEC Media Network:

> **Strictly No Emojis & Arrow Link Placement:** NortheastAEC™ maintains an authoritative architectural whitepaper aesthetic. Never use emojis (no pointing fingers, thumbs, or decorative symbols in text or links). For callouts and cross-references, always place a clean typographical arrow *before* the link pointing directly at it (e.g., `&rarr; [Guide Title](url)`), never trailing after the link text.
>
> **Strictly No Numbered Subtitles in Features or Reports:** Features and Reports must use clean descriptive architectural titles (e.g., `## The Convective Knee-Wall Loop`, `## Historic Mortar Compatibility Mechanics`). Do not prefix headings with arbitrary numbers (never use `## 1.`, `## 2.`). Numbering is strictly reserved for Listicles (numbered comparison items) and Tutorials (sequential execution steps).

### Format 1: Features (Profiles, Deep Dives & Archetypes)
- **Role:** Deep investigative breakdowns of regional architectural envelopes (e.g., fuel oil to heat pump decarbonization, Cape Cod freezing knee walls, historic fieldstone basement decay).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Scene $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Resolution.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** (`# Title`)
  2. **Description Subtitle** (`subtitle: "..."` in frontmatter)
  3. **Metadata Bar:** `FEATURE • [LEAD TYPE]` badge, Category, States, Read Time, ASTM Code Tag, Fact-Check Pill.
  4. **Quick Summary Box:** Styled callout container with executive summary text followed by 3–4 bulleted key takeaways with green checkmarks.
  5. **Descriptive or Staccato Lead:** Sets the hook immediately (e.g., *Sub-zero gale rattling the sash cords. The heating oil burner rumbles in the cellar, burning through seven gallons a day. Yet upstairs in the Cape Cod bedroom, your ankles freeze from a 15-degree convective floor draft...*).
  6. **Bold Medium Secondary Titles** (`## Section`)
  7. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph** throughout. Never write dense multi-sentence blocks.

### Format 2: Reports (News & Technical Analyses)
- **Role:** High-authority, data-first teardowns of energy efficiency retrofits, air-to-water heat pump COP curves, and historic preservation codes.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Fact First $\rightarrow$ Supporting Data & Physics $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Code/Economic Baseline.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Technical Diagram or Comparison Matrix** (placed high in the piece).
  3. **Data Teardown:** Empirical metrics (heating COP at 5°F and -15°F, compressive strength in PSI, vapor permeance perms, annual therms displacement).
  4. **Code Compliance Box:** Exact IECC Zone 5A/6A, Massachusetts Stretch Energy Code, or NYS Uniform Code citations.

### Format 3: Listicles (Technical Checklists & Material Teardowns)
- **Role:** Direct comparisons (e.g., *5 Cold-Climate Heat Pumps Rated for -15°F Sub-Zero Heat*, *6 Breathable Mortars for Historic Fieldstone*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Context $\rightarrow$ Numbered Items with Technical Subheadings $\rightarrow$ Buyer/Builder Verdict.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Metadata.**
  2. **Numbered H2 Headings:** `## 1. Material / Component Name` (Numbers permitted exclusively in Listicles).
  3. **Each item must include:** Technical metric table (Cost per unit, Temperature limit, Compressive strength, Energy rebate eligibility).

### Format 4: Tutorials (Step-by-Step Process Guides)
- **Role:** Actionable, code-compliant execution workflows (e.g., *How to Air Seal a Cape Cod Knee Wall*, *Repointing a Stone Foundation with Hydraulic Lime*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Overview $\rightarrow$ Code/Permit Requisites $\rightarrow$ Sequential Execution Steps $\rightarrow$ Inspection Checklist.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Quick Summary.**
  2. **Jobsite Requisites Table:** Tools, PPE, lime mixing ratios, electrical safety disconnects.
  3. **Numbered Steps:** `## Step 1: Mortar Raking & Joint Preparation`.
  4. **Pro-Tip & Safety Warning Callouts:** Emphasizing silica dust PPE (OSHA Table 1 compliant HEPA vacuums), lead paint abatement (RRP Rule), and lime mortar curing humidity control.

## 3. The "Empower First, Safety Net Second" Protocol

Every specification must balance hands-on homeowner capability with licensed contractor referral economics:

1. **Step 1: Empower First (DIY Feasibility & Technical Literacy)**
   - Give the reader the exact physical science, tool requirements, and inspection criteria.
   - Demystify the process so they understand exactly what needs to be done.
2. **Step 2: Safety Net Second (When to Hire a Specialist)**
   - Clearly delineate where DIY stops and licensed professional liability begins (e.g., replacing 100A main service panels, structural jacking of sagging center-beam timber columns, extensive slate roof scaffolding on 12:12 pitches).
   - Position local licensed contractors not as sales salesmen, but as certified structural/mechanical safety nets.
3. **Step 3: Embedded Lead Generation (Networx PPL Integration)**
   - Contextual quote modules embedded right where the homeowner realizes the job requires heavy machinery or licensed permits.
